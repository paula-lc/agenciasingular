How to get Google Maps and AI to recommend your business

The practical guide for nail artists, hair salons, florists, workshops, shops and cafés that want to be “the recommended place” on Google, on Maps and in Gemini, ChatGPT or Google's AI.

Summary in 30 seconds

  • Google Maps and AI recommend businesses about which they have clear, complete and consistent information, and that others speak well of.
  • The foundations are the same for both: a complete Google listing, reviews, a website with one page per service and the same details everywhere.
  • AI rewards content that answers specific questions: guide prices, opening hours, what makes you different, who it's for.
  • Measure: ask the AI assistants about your service in your area yourself once a month.

How Google Maps and AI decide who to recommend

Google explains that its local results depend mainly on three things: relevance (whether your listing matches what's being searched for), distance (how close you are) and prominence (how well known and well rated you are, online and offline). You can't change distance; you can change the other two.

AI assistants —Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot or Google's AI answers— work in a similar way: when someone asks “where can I get my nails done near here?”, they search the web, read business listings, websites, reviews and articles, and summarise what they find. If there's little information about you, or it's contradictory, they don't mention you. If there's plenty, and it's clear and positive, you're a candidate to appear.

That's why the work is the same for both: be easy to understand, easy to verify y easy to recommend.

Step 1 · Make your Google Maps listing spotless

The Google Business Profile is free and, by far, the most profitable piece for a neighbourhood business. Tick off what you already have:

  • Listing claimed and verified in your name (not an agency's).
  • Exact primary category (“Nail salon”, not just “Beauty salon”) and secondary categories.
  • Your business's real name, without added keywords (it's against Google's rules).
  • Up-to-date opening hours, including holidays and special hours.
  • Services or products with a description and guide price.
  • A natural description: what you do, who for, in which area and what sets you apart.
  • At least 10 real photos: shopfront (so people recognise you), interior, team and work.
  • Attributes filled in: accessible, wifi, terrace, online booking, card payments…
  • Link to your website and to bookings or orders.

Step 2 · Get reviews that work for you

Reviews influence Maps and are one of AI's favourite sources for deciding whether to recommend you. What counts: quantity, recency and what they say.

  • Ask for the review at the happy moment: when you hand over the bouquet, finish the manicure or return the car.
  • Put a QR code on the counter and send the direct link by WhatsApp after the visit.
  • Suggest they mention the service (“would you tell us what we did for you?”). Reviews that say “the best balayage in the neighbourhood” help people find you for “balayage”.
  • Reply to all of them, by name and in a friendly tone. To negative ones, calmly and offering a solution.
  • Never buy reviews or offer discounts in exchange: Google forbids it and may penalise your listing.

Step 3 · Have a website Google and AI understand

Instagram is great for making people fall in love, but AI and Google mostly read websites. Your website is the official source. For it to work:

  • One page for each important service (“Gel manicure”, “Bridal bouquets”, “Tyre change”) saying what it is, guide price, duration, who it's for and where.
  • Real FAQs, written the way your customers ask them. It's the format AI quotes most.
  • Real text, not images: the restaurant menu or the price list as text, not a scanned PDF or a photo.
  • Name, address, phone and opening hours visible in the footer of every page.
  • Structured data (schema.org types such as LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage…): an invisible “spec sheet” that helps machines read you.
  • Make it load fast on mobile and look good. Most local searches happen on mobile.

Step 4 · The same details everywhere

If Google shows one set of opening hours, Instagram another and an old directory still has your old phone number, Google and AI can't tell which is right. And when in doubt, they recommend someone else.

  • Write your name, address and phone exactly the same everywhere.
  • Create or review your listing on Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps and Siri) and Bing Places (Bing and Copilot).
  • Check the directories in your sector: TripAdvisor or TheFork for hospitality, Booksy or Treatwell for beauty, garage platforms, etc.
  • Google your business name and correct any outdated details that come up.

Step 5 · Get people talking about you

AI trusts a business more when other sources mention it. You don't need to be on TV: local presence is enough.

  • Traders' associations, markets and fairs in your neighbourhood.
  • Collaborations with other nearby businesses (the florist with the café next door).
  • Blogs, local media and city guides (“the best brunch spots in…”).
  • Local content creators who visit your business.

Step 6 · Measure what they say about you

Once a month, spend 15 minutes on this:

  1. Ask Gemini, ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode: “Which [your service] do you recommend in [your neighbourhood or city]?”. Note whether you appear and what they say.
  2. Also ask about yourself directly: “What do you know about [your business name]?”. If something's wrong, find out where it comes from and fix it.
  3. Check the performance of your Business Profile: calls, direction requests, website clicks.
  4. If you have a website, check Google Search Console: which searches you appear for.

Local SEO vs. GEO: how they're alike and how they differ

AspectLocal SEOGEO (AI visibility)
GoalRank at the top of Google and MapsGet the AI to name you in its answer
What the customer seesA list of results and a mapAn answer with 2 or 3 recommendations
What matters mostGoogle listing, reviews, website, distanceClear, consistent information across many sources, reviews and mentions
Ideal contentPages per service and areaDirect answers to specific questions
How it's measuredRankings, calls, visitsAsking AI assistants and tracking mentions

The bottom line: they're not two different jobs. Good local SEO is the foundation of GEO, and GEO adds a layer of clarity and reputation that also improves your SEO.

The 7 most common mistakes we see

  1. Having a Google listing created by someone else (or an agency) and no access to it.
  2. Choosing a generic category on Google Maps.
  3. Menu or prices only as a photo or PDF.
  4. A website with no service pages: everything on a single home page.
  5. Different opening hours on Google, Instagram and the website.
  6. Not replying to reviews (or replying badly to negative ones).
  7. Using stock photos instead of real photos of the business.

FAQ

What is GEO, or AI visibility?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the work of getting AI assistants such as Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews to understand your business, trust the information they find about it and mention it in their answers.

Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT or Gemini answers?

There's no way to pay for an AI to recommend you in its organic answers. What you can do is improve the sources they consult: your website, your Google listing, reviews and mentions elsewhere.

Is SEO still worth it if people already ask AI?

Yes. AI assistants with web search rely on search engine results and business listings. Good local SEO is the foundation of AI visibility, not a substitute for it.
